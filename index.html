What is gender-based violence?

It is harm done to someone because of their gender or power over them. It can happen to anyone, in any relationship, on or off campus. It is never your fault.

Physical violence

Hitting, slapping, choking, pushing, or any use of force that hurts you.

Sexual violence

Any sexual act without your free consent, including when you are afraid, pressured, drunk or asleep, and harassment or touching you do not want.

Emotional abuse

Threats, insults, constant checking of your phone, isolating you from friends, or controlling where you go.

Online abuse

Sharing private photos, threatening messages, stalking on social media, or pressure to send images.

Abuse of power

A lecturer, tutor or senior offering marks, accommodation or favours in exchange for sex, or punishing you for saying no.

Money control

Taking your allowance, stopping you from working, or making you hand over your money.

I’m not sure. Is what happened to me GBV?

If something made you feel scared, controlled, forced or ashamed, it matters, even if there is no injury and even if the person is someone you know or care about. You do not need a label to ask for support. A counsellor can talk it through with you.

Your options

The choice is yours. You can do one thing, several, or none yet. Nobody should pressure you.

  1. Talk to someone you trustA counsellor on the helpline, a campus counsellor, a friend or a family member. Being listened to helps.
  2. Get medical careTreatment for injuries, protection against pregnancy and infection, and a record of what happened if you decide to report later. Go to Angau Hospital’s Family Support Centre.
  3. Find a safe placeIf it is not safe where you are staying, Femili PNG can help arrange safe accommodation. Locations are confidential.
  4. Tell the universityYou can report to Student Affairs or the counselling office, who can help with a change of accommodation, extra time for assessments and protection from the person.
  5. Report to policeThe Family and Sexual Violence Unit handles these cases. A caseworker can go with you. You can take this step now, later, or not at all.

Stay private online

No website can promise to hide that you visited it. These steps lower the risk.

Use a private window

On most phones and browsers, open a “private” or “incognito” tab before visiting. This stops the page appearing in your history. It does not hide your activity from a network owner or someone watching your screen.

Clear your history afterwards

In your browser settings, clear history for the last hour or day. If you cannot, use someone else’s device you trust, or a quiet hour at a library.

Be careful with shared devices and campus Wi-Fi

Hostel computers and campus networks may be monitored. A phone on your own mobile data is safer. If you suspect someone has access to your phone, such as spyware or a shared account, call the helpline from a different phone.

Protect your messages

Turn off message previews on your lock screen. Delete call logs and SMS threads if it is safe to do so. Do not save the helpline under its real name if that could cause trouble.

Use the Quick exit button

The orange button at the top right of every page, and the Esc key, take you straight to an ordinary weather page and clear anything typed into the form on this page.

Supporting a friend

Listen and believe

Say “I believe you” and “It’s not your fault.” Do not ask why they did not leave or fight back.

Let them lead

Offer options, such as the helpline, a counsellor or medical care. Do not push them to report or confront the person.

Keep it private

Do not share their story without their permission. Offer to go with them to appointments.

Look after yourself

Supporting someone is hard. The helpline also supports friends and family.
